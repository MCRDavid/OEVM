# What operators send the government, and what is published about enforcement

Operators send the government regular reports on reliability, helplines and roaming, and must
give it their data on request. None of those reports has to be made public. On 17 October 2026
we found no published enforcement action under the Public Charge Point Regulations 2023, on any
of its requirements.

## What operators send the government

The Department for Transport's
[guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-public-charge-point-regulations-2023-guidance/public-charge-point-regulations-2023-guidance)
(updated 21 October 2024) lists the reports operators must send to the Secretary of State and the
enforcement authority:

| Report | How often | Public? |
|---|---|---|
| Reliability: the number of charge points, the reliability of the rapid network, and each charge point's time in each status | Every year; the first was due by 14 January 2026, covering 2025 | "This report does not need to be made publicly available" |
| Helpline: calls received, the help asked for, time to resolve and calls not resolved | Every quarter | "This report does not need to be made publicly available" |
| Roaming: which roaming providers drivers can pay through, and changes | Within 28 days of a change | "This information does not need to be publicly available" |

Regulation 10(2)(b) also requires operators to give their data "on request in electronic format
to the Secretary of State" and to the electricity network operators that supply their chargers
([regulation 10](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2023/1168/regulation/10/made)). For its
statistics, the department buys aggregated data from Zapmap (see
[the first post](findings/no-single-list/)).

## What operators publish instead

The one thing the guidance asks operators to publish is a statement on their reliability: they
"must publish information on their compliance with the reliability requirement on their website".
When we looked for open data, that was often all we found. Be.EV's regulations page covered
reliability only (10 October 2026). Connected Kerb's "Regulatory Data" link was a one-page PDF on
2025 reliability at its 6 rapid sites, and RAW Charging's regulations section covered 2025
reliability only (11 October 2026). TotalEnergies' UK page said its rapid network "was 99.3%
reliable for 2025", and Lidl's page gave 2025 uptime of 99%. None of those pages linked open data.

## Who enforces the regulations

The Office for Product Safety and Standards (OPSS), part of the Department for Business and
Trade, enforces the regulations for the Secretary of State. Its
[enforcement guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/opss-enforcement-enforcement-actions/alternative-fuels-infrastructure-regulations-and-public-charge-point-regulations)
(updated 8 August 2025) describes enforcement undertakings, compliance notices, which can also bar
an operator from installing more public charge points, and civil penalties of up to £10,000 for
each public charge point for each breach (for the reliability requirement, £10,000 for a network
of rapid charge points).
It says OPSS "publishes details of any Compliance Notice that we serve" and "publishes details of
any Civil Penalty that we impose", as well as the undertakings it accepts.

The Department for Transport's guidance adds: "The enforcement authority will take a pragmatic
approach to enforcement, opening dialogue in the first instance to understand the reasoning
behind any non-compliance."

## What we found published

Checked on 17 October 2026:

- OPSS's list of
  [enforcement undertakings](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/regulations-enforcement-undertakings-accepted-by-opss)
  (last updated 6 February 2026) has undertakings under the Electric Vehicles (Smart Charge
  Points) Regulations 2021 only. It lists none under the Public Charge Point Regulations 2023.
- OPSS's
  [enforcement action reports](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/opss-enforcement-actions)
  for 1 April 2024 to 31 March 2026 cover construction products, environmental protection,
  product safety and timber. None mentions charge points.

So we found no published compliance notice, civil penalty or undertaking under the 2023
Regulations. That does not show that no action has been taken: OPSS may be in dialogue with
operators without formal action, and reports for the months after March 2026 had not yet been
published. We also found no published figures on how many operators publish open data, or on
checks of its accuracy.

## Why it matters

The reports go to the government and stay there, and no enforcement under the regulations is
published. So a driver, a researcher or a new app has no public way to tell which operators
publish their data, whether what they publish matches what they report, or whether the open data
rule is being checked. This project's [transparency page](transparency/) records, for each
network, what we found and when, so that at least one public record exists.
