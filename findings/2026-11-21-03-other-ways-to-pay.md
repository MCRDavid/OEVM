# Other ways to pay: what the map can and cannot say

The price in an operator's feed is often not the cheapest way to pay at its chargers. From 15
November 2026 the details of eight networks' chargers say when paying in the operator's own app,
or with a membership or subscription, is cheaper, but not by how much, because those operators'
website terms do not allow their prices to be copied. A check of charge cards, roaming apps and car
makers' charging services on 21 November 2026 found more whose plans cannot be shown for the same
reason, and two whose plans now can.

## Why the map lists some plans and not others

On 9 October 2026 the project owner decided to list a provider's plans only where its own terms
allow its pages to be reused, and to ask the others in writing. On 21 November 2026 the terms of 12
providers allowed it, and 37 of their plans were listed at the networks they cover. For 28 more,
the terms restrict copying or allow personal use only, or could not be read. Those requests are
drafted but had not been sent by 21 November 2026. Wherever a provider's own network publishes open
data, its prices still show from that feed.

## A feed with only the contactless price

On 15 November 2026 the owner pointed out that evyve is cheaper to pay through its own app,
especially at weekends. evyve's pricing page, read that day, confirmed it: paying in its free app
costs less than paying by contactless, and less again at weekends and on bank holidays. evyve's
feed gives one price for each connector, with no tariff type and no limit on days or times. At 300
of its 307 DC connectors that price is the page's contactless price, and no tariff in the feed has
the app's prices. So the map was showing the dearer way to pay, and evyve's terms do not allow the
cheaper prices to be copied onto it.

## Saying "cheaper", without the price

The same day the owner decided that where an operator's own pages show that paying it directly is
cheaper than paying at the charger, its chargers' details say so, with whether a subscription is
needed, and send the visitor to its website or app. No price or amount is given, nothing is quoted
from the operator's pages, and the note says when the page was last checked. It is added only after
the feed has been compared with the page, so that "cheaper" is right where the map shows the price
at the charger.

Operators whose terms keep their prices off the map were checked against their own pages and feeds
on 15 November 2026. Eight got a note:

- evyve: paying in its app.
- IONITY: signing up in its app, and a subscription.
- bp pulse: a subscription. Paying as you go in its app costs the same as contactless.
- Jolt: its app, which gives some free charging each day.
- Source EV: a free membership, and a paid one, at its DC locations only.
- ESB Energy: a membership. Its feed already gives the pay as you go price, and contactless costs a
  little more.
- ESB ecars: a membership.
- ubitricity: a Shell Recharge subscription.

Mer UK did not get one, because a registered user saves very little, and only at locations on its
guest tariff, which the note cannot pick out. Neither did Arnold Clark Charge, whose subscription is
already listed with its price as an Octopus Electroverse plan, or RAW Charging, which publishes no
comparison. Allego's and Shell Recharge's pricing pages could not be read from the cloud, so they
still need checking in a browser.

Also from 15 November 2026, a visitor who knows a cheaper rate can type it in for a network, for
its AC chargers, its DC chargers or both. The map then uses it for that network's labels, marked as
the visitor's own. It is never sent anywhere, and it stays on the visitor's device only if they
choose to remember their settings.

## Charge cards and memberships, 21 November 2026

Each of these was read on the provider's own pages.

- **Octopus Electroverse** now lists on its home page the UK networks it works with, saying the
  list is not complete. Its pay as you go plan and its discounts for Octopus Go customers are now
  linked to the 42 networks on the list that have a record in this project, and show at the
  chargers of those on the map. Networks not on the list are not linked, although Electroverse may
  still work at some of them.
- **Bonnet is now OVO Charge Anywhere.** Bonnet's website redirects to a page on OVO's website, the
  app is now called OVO Charge, and one of Bonnet's two paid plans is no longer offered on the page.
  The terms still allow reuse, so the pay as you go plan and the remaining paid plan are listed
  under the new name.
- **Polestar Charge**, for Polestar drivers, is run by Plugsurfing on Plugsurfing's own website
  and under its terms, which allow reuse. Its two paid plans, discounts on DC charging at named
  networks, are now listed.
- **Kia Charge, BMW Charging and MB.CHARGE Public** are services of one company, Digital Charging
  Solutions. Their websites carry a copyright notice that claims database rights and forbids
  automated extraction of their data without the company's written consent, so their plans are not
  listed.
- **Mobilize Charge Pass**, Renault's charging service: Renault UK's website terms forbid copying
  its material, so its plans are not listed.
- **Volkswagen's We Charge**: its page names three plans, but their prices could not be read from
  the page itself, so none are listed.
- **Zapmap**'s terms, last updated in March 2026, still forbid text and data mining and
  republishing.
- Pages of Tesla, GeniePoint, ScottishPower and Allstar Chargepass refused automated reads, so they
  need checking in a browser.

Each provider's record, with the date its terms were checked, is in the [project's list of
providers](providers/README.md).
